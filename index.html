💗 What is breast cancer?

Breast cancer occurs when abnormal cells in the breast grow out of control and form a tumour. It can affect women and, less commonly, men. Different types can behave differently and may need different treatments.

Why awareness matters: knowing how your breasts or chest normally look and feel can help you notice a new or unusual change and seek appropriate medical advice.

✋ How to Check Your Breasts/Chest

There is no single perfect technique. The goal is to become familiar with what is normal for you and notice changes. You can check in a mirror, in the shower, or lying down—choose a comfortable method.

1

LOOK 👀

Stand in front of a mirror with your arms relaxed, then raise them. Look for a new change in size, shape, skin or nipple appearance.

2

FEEL ✋

Use the pads of your fingers, not the fingertips. Gently feel the breast/chest area using comfortable pressure so you can feel different depths without causing pain.

3

COVER THE WHOLE AREA ↔️

Feel across the whole breast/chest area, including toward the collarbone and into the armpit. A consistent pattern can help you cover everything.

4

NOTICE CHANGES 🩷

Pay attention to a new lump, swelling, skin change, nipple change, unusual discharge, or another persistent change that is different for you.

Important: breast awareness is not a diagnostic test and does not replace recommended screening. Most breast changes are not cancer, but a new or unusual change should be assessed by a healthcare professional.

🚨 Changes to Look Out For

⭕ New lump or swelling

In the breast/chest or armpit.

〰️ Skin changes

Dimpling, puckering, redness, thickening or another unusual change.

↪️ Nipple changes

New inward pulling, rash or another unusual change.

💧 Unusual discharge

Unexpected discharge, especially bloody discharge.

Also notice: a change in breast size or shape, persistent or unusual pain, or any change that is new or not normal for you.

Remember: a symptom does not automatically mean cancer. There are many non-cancerous causes of breast changes. The important step is getting a new or unusual change checked.

🌱 Looking After Your Breast Health

🏃 Stay active

Regular physical activity is associated with lower breast cancer risk.

⚖️ Healthy weight

Maintaining a healthy weight, particularly after menopause, can help reduce risk.

🥂 Limit alcohol

Alcohol is associated with increased breast cancer risk; less is better for risk reduction.

🚭 Don't smoke

Avoiding tobacco supports overall health and cancer prevention.

👨‍👩‍👧 Know family history

Tell a healthcare professional about close relatives with breast or ovarian cancer, especially at a young age.

🩺 Follow screening advice

Screening depends on age, personal risk, family history and local recommendations.

🩺 What About Screening?

Screening checks for breast cancer before symptoms appear. The recommended test and starting age vary by country and individual risk.

Do not wait for a screening appointment if you have a new or unusual symptom. Contact a healthcare professional for assessment.

❓ Common Questions

Does every lump mean cancer?

No. Many breast lumps are non-cancerous, but a new lump should be assessed rather than self-diagnosed.

Can young women get breast cancer?

Yes. It is less common at younger ages, but it can occur. Do not ignore a persistent or unusual change.

Can men get breast cancer?

Yes, although it is much less common.

Is breast pain always cancer?

No. Breast pain has many possible causes and is often not caused by cancer. Persistent or unusual pain should be discussed with a healthcare professional.
