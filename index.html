PINK OCTOBER • BREAST CANCER AWARENESS

Know Your Body.
Know the Signs.

You are strong. You are beautiful. You are worth caring for. Learn what is normal for you and speak up when something changes.

What is breast cancer?

Breast cancer happens when abnormal cells in the breast grow out of control and form a tumour. It can affect women and, less commonly, men. Early diagnosis and timely treatment can improve outcomes.

Know Your Normal 💗

There is no single “normal” breast. Breasts can differ in size and shape and can change with the menstrual cycle, pregnancy, breastfeeding, menopause, weight changes and some medicines. The goal is to notice a new or unusual change for you.

👀

1. Look

Stand in front of a mirror. Look with your arms by your sides, then raise them. Notice changes in size, shape, skin or nipples.

✋

2. Feel

Use the pads of your fingers to gently feel the breast/chest area. Use light and firmer pressure without pressing so hard that it hurts.

↔️

3. Cover the area

Feel around the whole breast/chest area, up toward the collarbone and into the armpit. You can do this in the shower or lying down if easier.

🩷

4. Check the nipples

Notice a new change in nipple shape, an inward pull, rash or unusual discharge. Do not squeeze the nipple to “test” it.

Important: Breast awareness is not a diagnostic test. Most breast changes are not cancer, but a new or unusual change should be assessed by a healthcare professional.

🚨 Changes to Look Out For

  • A new lump or swelling in the breast, chest or armpit
  • A change in breast size or shape
  • Skin dimpling, puckering, redness or other unusual skin changes
  • A new change in the nipple, including inward pulling or a rash
  • Unusual nipple discharge, especially if bloody
  • A persistent or unusual change or pain

Not every symptom means cancer. If something is new or unusual for you, arrange an evaluation with a healthcare professional.

🌱 Caring for Your Breast Health

Move your body

Regular physical activity can help lower breast cancer risk.

Know your family history

Tell a healthcare professional if close relatives have had breast or ovarian cancer, especially at a young age.

Limit alcohol

Alcohol use is associated with increased breast cancer risk; less is better for risk reduction.

Follow screening advice

Screening recommendations depend on age, personal risk and local guidelines. Ask a healthcare professional which schedule is right for you.

💕 Your health deserves your attention.

If you notice a new or unusual change, don't ignore it. Get it checked.
